# Before/After Examples

Examples 1 to 5 are Hardik Pandya's. Examples 6 to 13 are Gaurav Tiwari's. All of them are illustrations of a pattern; none of the numbers or products in them is a claim to reuse.

## Example 1: Throat-Clearing + Binary Contrast

**Before:**
> "Here's the thing: building products is hard. Not because the technology is complex. Because people are complex. Let that sink in."

**After:**
> "Building products is hard. Technology is manageable. People aren't."

**Changes:** Removed opener, binary contrast structure, and emphasis crutch. Direct statements.

---

## Example 2: Filler + Unnecessary Reassurance

**Before:**
> "It turns out that most teams struggle with alignment. The uncomfortable truth is that nobody wants to admit they're confused. And that's okay."

**After:**
> "Teams struggle with alignment. Nobody admits confusion."

**Changes:** Cut hedging ("most"), removed throat-clearing phrases, deleted permission-granting ending.

---

## Example 3: Business Jargon Stack

**Before:**
> "In today's fast-paced landscape, we need to lean into discomfort and navigate uncertainty with clarity. This matters because your competition isn't waiting."

**After:**
> "Move faster. Your competition is."

**Changes:** Eliminated jargon entirely. Core message in six words.

---

## Example 4: Dramatic Fragmentation

**Before:**
> "Speed. Quality. Cost. You can only pick two. That's it. That's the tradeoff."

**After:**
> "Speed, quality, cost: pick two."

**Changes:** Single sentence. No performative emphasis.

---

## Example 5: Rhetorical Setup

**Before:**
> "What if I told you that the best teams don't optimize for productivity? Here's what I mean: they optimize for learning. Think about it."

**After:**
> "The best teams optimize for learning, not productivity."

**Changes:** Direct claim. No rhetorical scaffolding.

---

## Example 6: Pricing Copy

**Before:**
> "Unlock the full power of our platform with our Pro plan. It's a game-changer for teams looking to navigate the complexities of modern collaboration. Whether you're a solo founder or a growing organisation, this tier delivers everything you need to take your workflow to the next level."

**After:**
> "Pro: 10 seats, unlimited projects, priority support. $29 per seat per month."

**Changes:** 47 words to 12. Kept the four facts a buyer compares: seats, project limit, support level and price. Cut significance inflation ("unlock the full power"), jargon ("game-changer"), gerund filler ("navigate the complexities") and false comprehensiveness ("whether you're X or Y").

---

## Example 7: Generic AI Claim

**Before:**
> "In today's fast-paced digital landscape, leveraging AI can be a transformative way to elevate your content strategy and unlock new levels of engagement."

**After:**
> "AI gets a first draft onto the screen faster. I still own the claims, examples, and final edit."

**Changes:** 25 words carrying one idea became two sentences that name the useful gain and the work the tool cannot own. Removed the landscape opener and four hype verbs.

---

## Example 8: Error Message

**Before:**
> "An unexpected error has occurred. Please try again later."

**After:**
> "We couldn't save your changes. Try again, or copy your work and refresh."

**Changes:** Says what failed and what the user can do now. "Unexpected" and "later" told the user nothing.

---

## Example 9: Caption With Production Commentary

**Before:**
> "A fresh screenshot of the settings screen, captured for this guide because a real interface beats another generic mockup."

**After:**
> "The Permalinks screen with Post name selected."

**Changes:** A hard fail. The original caption talked about how the image was made and why it was chosen. The new one says what the reader is looking at.

---

## Example 10: Roundup Filler

**Before** (closing three different plugin entries):
> "It's a good fit if your needs match its feature set. Check the official site for current pricing before you buy."

**After** (one entry):
> "Skip it if you already run a page builder. The two overlap on most layout features."

**Changes:** The original closer fit every item, so it informed about none. The swap test caught it. The general buying advice moved to the intro, once.

---

## Example 11: Performed Humanity

**Before:**
> "Look, I tried it. Actually, wait. Scratch that. It's... fine? Honestly? Nope."

**After:**
> "It works, but the setup took longer than the tool it replaced."

**Changes:** The fragments and fake corrections were there to sound human. They carried no thought. One plain judgment with its reason does the job.

---

## Example 12: The Number Nobody Measured

**Before:**
> "After a year, the options table holds 50,000 expired rows and every admin page waits 3 seconds."

**After:**
> "Expired rows pile up in the options table and slow down every admin page."

**Changes:** The numbers looked measured but weren't. The mechanism is what the reader needs. Keep exact figures only when they are real.

---

## Example 13: The Right Edit Is No Edit

**Writer's sentence:**
> "It is wise to keep one caching plugin on a site and switch the rest off."

**Over-edited:**
> "Use only one caching plugin."

**Right call:** leave the original alone.

**Changes:** None. "It is wise" is a measured, teacherly full form, and "the rest off" tells the reader what to do with the others. The shorter version is not clearer. It is only flatter. Stop Slop removes predictable patterns; it does not erase the writer.
